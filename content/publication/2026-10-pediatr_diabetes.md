abstract = "This paper evaluates deep learning models for blood glucose prediction in children and adolescents with type 1 diabetes using insulin pumps and investigates whether transfer learning from adult data improves prediction accuracy and safety. Models were trained using data from the Type 1 Diabetes Exercise Initiative Pediatric study (84 pediatric insulin pump users) and its adult counterpart (380 adult insulin pump users). Two transfer learning strategies were compared: one using pediatric data only and another pre-training the model on adult data before fine-tuning it on the pediatric cohort and each target patient. Prediction accuracy and clinical safety were assessed using root mean square error (RMSE) and the Clarke Error Grid (CEG) at 10-, 20-, and 30-min prediction horizons, with results analyzed separately for open-loop and closed-loop users. Pre-training on adult data yielded the best performance. At 30 min, mean RMSE was 19.61 mg/dL (1.09 mmol/L), with at least 92.64% of predictions falling within zone A of the CEG for 75% of patients. No predictions fell within the unsafe zone E. Delayed hypoglycemia detection occurred only in a small subset of patients and was mainly associated with missing contextual information or atypical clinical events. An additional evaluation on pediatric patients treated with multiple daily injections (MDI) demonstrated clinically acceptable prediction accuracy and safety, supporting the applicability of the proposed transfer learning approach to MDI users. These findings demonstrate that transfer learning from adult data effectively mitigates pediatric data scarcity and enables accurate, clinically safe blood glucose prediction under free-living conditions."
